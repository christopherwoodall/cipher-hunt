### 1. Interference verdicts (no vetoes; one adverse re-grade, two watch-items)

- **06="ent" (iff pre=82, conditioned LEAD F61): NO VETO.** The obvious
  German rival — the hyper-frequent German prefix "ent-" (entdecken,
  entsprechen…) — is inapplicable BY CONSTRUCTION: the F61 conditioning
  pins 06="ent" to post-82 position, and a German prefix never occurs
  post-"m". The reading's live frame is the 94-82-06 "-ment" tail
  (94="ne" + 82=m + 06="ent"; cf. 77-78-94-82-06 = "gouvernement").
  German "-ment" loanwords (/mɛnt/, audible t: Parlament 18×, Moment 15×,
  Instrument 14×, Fundament 3×, Reglement 3× in AZ) are a banked
  ALTERNATIVE phonetic frame for the tail if the French /mɑ̃/ frame ever
  fails — the AZ gives the conditioner a ready German-lexicon control set.
  Not a rival today; no status change.
- **78="ver" (iff next=94, conditioned islet LEAD F38): NO VETO, small
  positive.** German "ver-" is /fɛʁ/ — a German thinker transliterating by
  ear would write "fer", not "ver". The cipher's v-spelling at 78 is
  evidence the writer was operating in FRENCH phonetics at this cell, i.e.
  evidence AGAINST German phonetic interference here, and it disfavors any
  future "German ver-prefix intrusion" rival for the gouvernement formula.
  The 77="gouv"/78="er" rival split is unnatural in both languages
  (German syllabifies Gou-ver-ne-ment, same as French) — no German leg for
  either side of the {ver,er} fork. Watch-item banked: if the 77/78-hunter
  ever finds a STANDALONE 78="er" window outside the formula, German "er"
  (= he/him, extremely frequent) becomes a live rival to French
  er-final-syllable readings — flag it then, not now.
- **48 verb-stems ("on 48"×6, "48 pas"×2; round-9 fresh battery): NO VETO
  possible yet (no specific stem claimed).** German check at the frame
  level: "on 48" is safe (French "on" = German "man"; a German thinker
  reaches for "on" naturally); "48 pas" has no German path (German negation
  is "nicht", never "pas") — a German verb stem between "ne"-frame and
  "pas" is implausible unless the whole negation frame is misread.
  RECOMMENDATION to the 48-battery executor: include German verb stems as
  rival controls (sag-, mach-, geh-, komm-, hab-, wird-, sei-/ist-, denk-,
  sprech-, schreib-) — the German slip is the specific alternative to a
  French stem, and German infinitives' "-en" gives a testable ending
  prediction if 48's successors pattern that way.
- **67's @1248 X ("pour [67] que", NEITHER et nor veut): no German word fits
  the frame better than French candidates — null result, one conditional.**
  German "dafür, daß" needs NO intervening word ("pour que" alone), so
  German interference mildly predicts the X is French-internal ("ce" /
  "autant" / "peu" — "pour ce que", "pour autant que", "pour peu que" all
  grammatical). Germanism WATCH-ITEM for the 67-finisher: if X ever resolves
  to "cela/ça", that is a "dafür daß" calque ("pour cela que" is
  ungrammatical French) — i.e. evidence FOR German interference, not
  against the window. Bank the conditional; do not treat "cela" at 67 as a
  clean French reading without addressing it.
